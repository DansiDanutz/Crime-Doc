# WRITING_STYLE — quick reference (derived from DNA §4)

Use this as the checklist while drafting a script. Full rules in `dna/CHANNEL_DNA.md`.

## The shape of every script
```
COLD OPEN  → CONTEXT  → MECHANISM (step by step)  → AFTERMATH  → CLIFFHANGER
~30-40 w      expand     the procedural core          implication   ≤4 sentences
```

## Cold open (memorize the formula)
> exact date + exact location + one concrete physical action.
- Do NOT name the topic yet. Drop the viewer into a moment.
- Then ONE anchor line: "This is the story of…" / "This is how he was caught."

Examples of the move:
- "It's the 13th of July in Butler, Pennsylvania…"
- "On the morning of July 5th, 2017, a man sits in front of his laptop in his villa in Bangkok."

## Sentence rhythm
- Short declaratives, 5–12 words. Chain them.
- Present tense = the unfolding now. Past tense = background/context.
- One ultra-short impact line where it lands hard ("It works." / "No answer.").
- One longer expository sentence per 4–6 short ones.
- Numbers, always: `400 ft or 120 m`, `$23 million`, `9:41 am`, `269 people`.
- Adjectives describe physical things and numbers — not feelings.

## Tone
Deadpan, clinical, calm — even over horror. Address the viewer only at pivots. Dry irony
on absurd facts is allowed; sarcasm and moralizing are not. One striking line per ~200 w.

## Pivots & devices
- Rhetorical question to turn an act: "What happened?" / "But where is he?"
- Lists of three.
- The load-bearing tangent (the saved email, the umbrella tip, the unlocked laptop).
- Procedural reconstruction with exact timing ("3 to 4 seconds after the first shot…").

## Endings (pick ONE, last line ≤12 words)
1. Unresolved present — the threat never ended.
2. Implicating turn — pivot to the viewer/society.
3. Next-mystery tease.
4. Unanswered "why" / "we don't know."
5. Lingering image — the cold-open image, recharged.

Land on a noun, name, date, or flat declarative. **Never resolve cleanly.**

## Banned
"In this video…" · "Today we're going to…" · "Have you ever wondered…" · INSANE /
shocking / you won't believe · tragically / horrifically / devastatingly · any sponsor
copy or "thanks for watching" · tidy happy endings.

## Length math
`words = minutes × 60 × 2.5 = minutes × 150`

| 3 | 450 |
| 5 | 750 |
| 8 | 1,200 |
