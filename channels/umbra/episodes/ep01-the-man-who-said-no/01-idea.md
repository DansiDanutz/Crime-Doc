# STATE 2 — Idea

**Working title:** The Man Who Said No

**Subject:** On 26 September 1983, Soviet duty officer Stanislav Petrov judged a satellite
missile-attack warning to be a false alarm — and was right.

**Why it fits the DNA:** Pure "system whose goals are not aligned with human well-being."
A lone red figure overrules the black institutional machine. Intelligence + infrastructure
failure + the Cold War. Procedural, numerate, unresolved.

**Cold-open moment:** Just after midnight, 26 September 1983, a bunker south of Moscow —
a man pours tea from a steel thermos and sits down facing a wall of screens.

**The mechanism:** The Oko early-warning satellites mistake sunlight glinting off
high-altitude clouds for missile launches → the panel reads LAUNCH, then five inbound
warheads → launch-on-warning doctrine demands he report a confirmed attack up the chain →
he reports a malfunction instead → ground radar shows an empty sky → he was right.

**Ending type:** 2 — implicating turn. The same kind of system still sits in every nuclear
state, faster and more trusted than the human in the chair.

**Key numbers/specifics:** 26 Sept 1983; ~00:14; one then five missiles; ~23 minutes to
impact; KAL 007 shot down 3 weeks earlier killing 269; satellite system "Oko" (eye);
bunker "Serpukhov-15"; dies 2017; ~1,000 warheads in a counterstrike.
